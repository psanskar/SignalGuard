A common job scam involves asking applicants to pay money before they can obtain employment.

A recruiter or supposed employer may request:

- Registration fees
- Application fees
- Processing fees
- Training fees
- Recruitment fees
- Security deposits
- Job placement fees
- Equipment deposits
- Money before an interview or job offer

Warning phrases may include:

- "Pay a registration fee"
- "Pay a processing fee"
- "Pay before starting"
- "Deposit money to secure your position"
- "Pay for training"
- "Send a fee before receiving the job"
- "Make an upfront payment"

Legitimate employers generally should not require applicants to send money simply to obtain a job.

## Safety Guidance

Do not pay a recruiter or employer just because they claim a fee is required to obtain employment.

Before making any payment, independently verify the company, recruiter, job advertisement, and reason for the payment.

Be especially cautious when payment requests are combined with urgency, guaranteed income, cryptocurrency transfers, or requests for sensitive personal or financial information.

An upfront payment request is a warning sign, but additional context should be considered before concluding that a job is fraudulent.
